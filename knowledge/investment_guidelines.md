# Investment Construction Guidelines

Use diversified, low-cost instruments as the core of a portfolio. Match the asset mix to
the investor's time horizon and ability to tolerate losses. A longer horizon can support
more equity exposure, while a short horizon calls for preserving capital and reducing
volatility.

Do not treat recent performance as a guarantee of future returns. Use monthly return and
volatility data as context, not as a prediction. Avoid allowing one asset to dominate the
portfolio, and explain material concentration or data-quality risks.

Gold can diversify equity risk but does not generate business cash flows. Broad-market
ETFs generally provide more diversification than a single company. Individual stocks
should be limited unless the investor explicitly accepts additional company-specific risk.

Recommendations are decision support, not guaranteed returns or personalized regulated
financial advice. State important assumptions and encourage the investor to verify costs,
taxes, liquidity, and suitability before investing.
